# Apple→Plate encoder study v1: results (TASK-062)

**Outcome O-ENC-ARCH.** The protocol (§10) defines this row as: no arm passes, every arm was
evaluated, and at least one arm succeeds on the bars without being spurious. **The preregistered
abandonment clause fires.**

- **No arm passes.**
- **A-tok** (the E0 recipe retrained on held-out roots, read at its patch tokens) meets every
  TASK-059 bar and passes the apple-hidden check. Its qualifier is **"meets the bars, not the
  floor"**:
  - it reads the apple to 0.463 cm with dx sign 178/190;
  - its median error is below its random-init floor F-tok;
  - but F-tok's own dx sign is 179/190. The floor rule requires a higher T2, so A-tok does not
    beat its floor.
- **A-sig, A-rec and A-plain** do not meet the bars, and none beats its floor. These are the three
  changes to the pooled 128-d latent.
- **Holm rejects nothing.** Every arm's p_arm is 1, because a point condition fails for each.

**What this is and is not.**
- An offline **readability** result: kernel-ridge readouts, 10-fold CV, 190 roots, features from
  encoders that never saw the root they read.
- **Not** a controller, and not a statement about closed-loop behaviour.

**Status lines.**
- **Learned Apple→Plate is still 0 successes.** No controller was trained or run.
- **The test split of `apple-wide-v1` was never decoded.** Training decoded only the
  G-split-checked train episodes. `DatasetStore.verify` read other episodes' bytes only to check
  their sha256.
- **Cohorts C (45300–45339) and D (45000–45007, 45100–45107) were never simulated.**
- **`exemption_spent` stays `false`.** No control formulation was preregistered.

## 5. Reading (interpretation, not measurement)

1. **The apple survives in the ViT's patch tokens and is lost at the pooled 128-d feature.** E-pool
   is supported on the reported-only comparisons below. A-tok, the arm that decides it, did not
   pass.
   - The recipe retrained on held-out roots reads the apple from its tokens: A-tok, 0.463 cm and
     178/190. It does not read it from its pooled feature: R0-cls, 1.223 cm and 162/190.
   - The difference is −0.759 cm [−0.931, −0.585].
   - Frozen E0 shows the same thing: E0-tok 0.415 cm against L-E0 1.273 cm, a difference of
     −0.858 cm [−1.046, −0.599]. Its E0 held-out caveat is that E0 saw the 170 train roots.
   - A-tok − L-raw is −0.006 cm [−0.068, 0.074]: no detectable difference from raw pixels. No
     equivalence margin was preregistered, so this is not a claim of equivalence.
2. **The gain over random init is not established under the preregistered floor rule.**
   - Random-init tokens (F-tok) also meet every bar: 0.601 cm and 179/190.
   - A-tok's median error is lower than F-tok's by 0.138 cm [0.062, 0.211].
   - On the dx sign, A-tok is one root behind: 178 against 179, discordance 1 against 2.
   - The protocol requires both a lower median and a higher T2, so A-tok does not beat its floor.
     That is the reason for the row, not a close call on the bars.
3. **None of the three changes to the pooled latent helped.**
   - Anti-collapse regularisation of the image feature (A-sig) raised the training-frame effective
     rank from about 8 to about 18. But the post-look rank stayed low (3.3 / 3.7), and the readout
     got worse: +0.466 cm against R0-cls.
   - Pixel reconstruction (A-rec) was no different from R0-cls: +0.042 cm [−0.165, 0.242].
   - Removing the privileged heads (A-plain) was worse than R0-cls: +0.326 cm [0.104, 0.466]. So
     E-supervision, as posed, is not supported. On these frames the heads help the pooled feature
     slightly; they do not hurt it.
   - So E-collapse, E-objective and E-supervision are not supported as fixes to the **pooled**
     latent.
4. **R0-cls reproduces E0's failure on held-out roots with half the data, on position.** Its
   median T1 is −0.050 cm [−0.264, 0.206] against L-E0, and it fails the bars as E0 does.
   - On the dx sign, R0-cls is better than L-E0: 162 against 147, McNemar 27 / 12, p = 0.024.
   - With one training run per half, the failure looks like a property of the recipe's pooled
     latent more than of E0's particular run, but that is not established.
5. **What this does not show.**
   - Whether a token-latent world model would carry the apple through prediction. No predictor was
     tested on tokens.
   - Anything about closed-loop behaviour.
   - Seed variance: there was one training run per arm and half.
   - E-distribution, the shared confound (§3.2 of the protocol), and capacity and input handling.
     Those remain untested.

## 6. The abandonment clause (protocol §10)

Quoted in full from the protocol, then the consequence for the corpus.

- **What closes.** O-ENC-ARCH fires the clause. The line *"train a LeWM-family encoder on
  `apple-wide-v1` train-split frames so that its frozen features expose the post-look apple"* is
  closed. No further readout-point, regularisation, objective or supervision variant of the
  TASK-054 recipe is preregistered on this corpus without new evidence of a different kind.
- **Recorded as untested, not refuted:** capacity, input handling and the shared E-distribution
  confound. They are argued against (§3.2), and this closure is limited by that. The recommended
  next step (an encoder that was not trained on this corpus) does not depend on them.
- **What does not close:** the LeWM backend, the encoder as a component, and the product goal.
- **The look-prefix corpus is not collected.** The owner's precondition was that some frozen
  encoder exposes the post-look apple, beyond its random init, under this protocol. It is not met.

## 7. Recommended next task (a recommendation; the task owner chooses)

Exactly one, as the O-ENC-ARCH row pre-declares.

**A preregistered test of an externally pretrained frozen encoder against its own random-init
floor, on this probe, before any corpus.**
- Same frames, readouts, bars, floor rule, Holm and apple-hidden check. No cross-fitting is

- It needs its own licence and provenance review (`docs/DEPENDENCIES.md`) and its own

- If it too fails to beat its floor, protocol §10 says that no look-prefix corpus is collected. The

  a **hardware/workspace change** on the robot.

The owner's decision may also want this reported-only fact: token features of this architecture,
including random-init ones, expose the post-look apple. The protocol gives it no row.

## 8. Caveats kept in the record

- **The floor rule's T2 condition is strict at the ceiling.** F-tok is at 179/190, so beating it
  needed 180/190, which is L-raw's own count. This was stated in advance ("F-tok may well succeed
  on its own, which would make A-tok's floor hard to beat") and is not re-litigated here.
- **E0-tok is not held out.** E0 trained on the 170 train roots and was selected on val. It is a
  diagnostic, and it has no decisional weight.
- **Each encoder saw about half the train roots.** e(A): 84 roots, 85 627 frames. e(B): 86 roots,
  89 824 frames. E0 saw 175 451 frames.
- **Kernel-ridge values are not bit-reproducible across machines** (BLAS). MPS training is not
  bit-deterministic. The encoder digests are in the manifest.
- **The out-of-fold predictions come from ten readouts**, so the Wilson intervals assume
  independence only approximately, as in TASK-059 and TASK-061.
- **The timing smoke that sized the caps ran an earlier, uncommitted runner** on smaller halves. The
  run's own per-encoder times (3364–3669 s) confirm the projection.
