# Apple→Plate information ceiling v1: results (TASK-059)

**Outcome O-OCC-NONE.** The protocol (§13) defines this row as: no decisional source succeeds on
all roots, and none succeeds on the visible stratum. **The 112 px reset frame, read out by
the preregistered readouts, does not give the apple position or the expert's step-0 `right_dx`
beyond what a prior already gives, on any stratum.**

That verdict splits into two parts.

- **Occlusion explains the occluded roots.** On 111 of 190 resets the right wrist hides the apple.
  On those resets every source sits at the prior, as §2.3 predicted before the run.
- **Occlusion does not explain the failure as a whole.** On the 79 roots where the apple *is*
  visible, no decisional 112 px source beats the visibility-aware prior either:
  - raw-112 pixels reach 1.658 cm, a ratio of 0.964 [0.751, 1.207] to that prior;
  - frozen E0 reaches 2.067 cm, *worse* than the prior's 1.720 cm.

  At 112 px the stored frames carry little apple-position signal that these readouts can use,
  even when the apple is in view.

## 6. Reading (interpretation, not measurement)

1. **The outcome row is O-OCC-NONE, and the camera is the problem.** Within this protocol,
   resolution cannot be separated from placement. Two things fail at once:
   - On 111/190 resets the apple is behind the right wrist. There, every source is at the prior,
     as §2.3 predicted before any readout existed.
   - On the 79 visible resets, the 112 px frame is not read out beyond a visibility-aware prior
     by any decisional source, **including raw pixels** (ratio 0.964 [0.751, 1.207]). A visible
     apple at 112 px covers 1–21 px of 12 544 (median 5 over the 79 visible resets). **So hiding explains the
     occluded roots, but not the failure as a whole.**
2. **E0 discards some position information, but that is not what binds here.** E0 is worse than
   raw-112 on T1: +0.296 cm [0.110, 0.475] overall and +0.409 cm [0.180, 0.746] on visible roots.
   On visible roots it is worse than the visibility-aware prior (2.067 cm against 1.720 cm). It is
   better than a random encoder of the same architecture (−0.212 cm [−0.383, −0.045]).
   - Even the raw pixels it could have preserved do not clear the prior. So a better encoder on
     the same 112 px frame would not, on this evidence, have supplied the step-0 command.
3. **The 448↓ result is a hypothesis, not a finding.** On visible roots the box-downsampled
   native render reads the apple much better than the stored 112 px frame (0.946 cm, ratio 0.550;
   apple x 0.568 cm; derived dx sign 67/79).
   - It is non-decisional, because it is **not** the stored-frame pipeline. The stored frame is
     4× MSAA at 112 px; 448↓ is a 16-sample box filter. §2.4 showed that, even after bias correction, the two differ
     per frame by more than the closest pair of resets does (0.130–0.212 against 0.038 levels).
   - It still misses both bars.
   - One more confound: 448↓ features are unrounded float box means, while the stored frames are
     uint8, so quantisation is also a candidate. Raw-448 native (1.482 cm), which carries the same
     information, reads worse than 448↓ (0.946 cm), which points at the readout's sensitivity to
     representation.
   - The hypothesis it raises: when the object covers a handful of pixels, anti-aliasing quality,
     effective resolution or quantisation matters. It is untested.
4. **The step-0 command is decided before the apple can be seen, but it becomes visible within a
   few steps.** Along the expert's own path the apple is visible by step 9 on every train root
   (median step 3). On 100/170 roots that happens before the x command has converged.
   - That makes a look-first strategy *possible*. The strategy: a reset-independent motion or
     pose that clears the wrist before the first apple-dependent command.
   - It is not shown to be *sufficient*, because of item 1: at 112 px even a visible apple was not
     read out beyond the prior.
5. **What this does not show.**
   - It does not show that no encoder or readout could extract the apple from these frames. Only
     the preregistered ridge readouts were tested, at n = 190.
   - It says nothing about closed-loop behaviour.
   - It does not test the carry failure that TASK-057 found after grasping.

## 7. Recommended next task (a recommendation; the task owner chooses)

**A joint, preregistered re-probe of the reset-time observation, with this frozen probe reused
unchanged** (targets, readouts, thresholds, baselines, folds and guards). Its arms vary the
observation along two axes:

1. **What is visible**, i.e. a look-first reset. For example:
   - a reset pose with the right hand raised or retracted out of the apple's line of sight; or
   - a short reset-independent "look" motion of at most the first ~10 commands, sized from §5.
     The apple is visible by step 9 on every train root.
2. **How many apple pixels there are**:
   - the existing `overview` camera, which sees the apple on 190/190 resets (≈ 12 px at 112 px,
     ≈ 185 px at 448 px);
   - a native higher-resolution or closer camera, rendered through a validated pipeline, so that
     the §2.4 confound does not recur;
   - optionally, a cheap rendering-path arm: a 112 px supersampled render, rounded and unrounded,
     to test the §6.3 hypothesis. Suggested at review; the owner decides.

The arms should cross the two axes, so the re-probe can say which one binds.

- **Named risk:** look-first alone may not be enough. On this run's visible roots, 112 px onboard
  frames were not read out beyond a visibility-aware prior (§6.1).
- **Order:** only an arm that passes this probe should justify new corpus collection or any
  control formulation.
- **Optional follow-up:** the apple-only shadow ablation that the §10 ruling deferred. It did not
  matter here, because §10 was never triggered.

The carry failure after a grasp (TASK-057 §5, item 2) remains a separate question for later.

- **The §10 shadow-off re-render is uninformative by construction** (the ruling in §1). It was
  not triggered.
- **CV out-of-fold predictions come from ten models**, so the Wilson intervals assume
  independence only approximately. There is no multiplicity correction across four sources and
  three strata (declared in §7 of the protocol).
- **Kernel-ridge values are not bit-reproducible across machines** (BLAS).
- **Only the preregistered readout families were tested**: linear and RBF kernel ridge. No CNN
  was trained (protocol §6).
- **The pre-freeze calibration looked at occlusion before the protocol froze.** This is disclosed
  in the protocol; that calibration fitted nothing.
- **Tie-break.** Exact inner-CV ties are broken by listed family, then by the smallest λ,
  as declared in PR #49.
