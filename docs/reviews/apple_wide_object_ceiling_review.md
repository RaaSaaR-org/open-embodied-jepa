## Correctness read-through

- **Twin and parity.** `ObjectRolloutModel.rollout` restores the snapshot, then projects and executes each step through the twin's unchanged `project_candidates`/`execute`. It breaks on the first rejection and records features right after `execute`. The robot-state first step is taken from `qpos[qadr]`, `qvel[vadr]` as float32. That is identical to the parent's `twin.observe().robot_state`, because `sim.read()` does not mutate data. The full-state list (`qpos`/`qvel` of the whole model, apple included) rotates in `encode` alongside the parent's list. Both are attached to the first rollout diagnostic of the next command, so there is one check per command after the first (387/388 in the smoke). `embodiment._grasp` is write-only, so resetting it in `_restore` cannot desynchronize the twin. The grasp rate state lives in `sim.targets`, which is restored.
- **Phase machine.** `_advance` loops over satisfied transitions on one live state. A phase entered in that loop starts with an empty progress history, so it cannot be blocked immediately. The blocked test needs more than 10 samples, and `history[-11] − history[-1] < 2 mm`. For descend it is gated on palm xy within 1 cm of the live grasp point, for transport on the apple within 4 cm of the plate, and for lower it always applies. Close and release are fixed-count phases, and retreat has no exit. `phase_stall` is checked after the transitions against the current phase's own count. The drop check precedes everything. `step_limit` matches the worker's loop limit of 1,000.
- **Costs.** Units are metres plus 0.2 × radians. Signs are correct: every term is a nonnegative distance or hinge, and lower is better. Anchors are taken at phase entry. `rotation_error` is the true geodesic angle, from `trace(Rᵀ·T)`. `candidate_costs` implements the TASK-044 rejection convention: 1,000 plus the last reached step cost, with the live-state cost used when step 0 is rejected. The test checks this.
- **Planner.** Hold and warm are candidates 0 and 1. Noise is clipped to phase bounds, and the grasp dims are pinned through `lower[12:] = upper[12:] = (−1, grasp)`, which assigns both arrays. Infeasible candidates are set to +inf, and ties go to the lowest index. The mean for round 2 is the best requested sequence so far. This is a one-elite iterated random search rather than a textbook CEM. The warm start is the shifted projected sequence, and `_enter` clears it on every phase change, before the bounds for the new phase are built. The RNG is `default_rng(reset seed)` and consumes a fixed amount of randomness per command, so the arm is deterministic (the smoke reproduced the probe's commands).
- **Acknowledgement and freshness.** `step` refuses to run while a command is pending, and refuses stale or equal observation timestamps. `acknowledge` requires the exact projected command and an advancing clock. A rejected execution sets `execution_rejected`.
- **Guard handling (ceiling).** Only the exact string `measured joint velocity limit exceeded` (`embodiment.py:363`) becomes a counted `guard_refused`. Every other projector `ContractError` is re-raised. That is the TASK-046 R1 lesson applied correctly.
- **Worker.** `scripted_oracle` calls `oracle.advance(result)` right after `execute`, and `policy_complete` fires before an extra observation is used. Full-state parity counters are initialized to 0 and incremented per diagnostic row. `object_controller.summary()` and the oracle phase are merged after `finally`. `privileged.close()` runs in `finally`. Object modes are refused by `make_plan` for other goal kinds and by the worker (`evaluate_apple.py:1897`). `last_goal_index` is not written for object plans.
- **Isolation.** Nothing is registered in `MODELS` (tested). Both classes require the acknowledgement flag. Retrieval (`retrieve_demonstration`, the frozen sensor) is unchanged. Truth enters only the ceiling's features, costs and transitions, and the oracle's labelled initial truth. No model input receives it.
- **Gate arithmetic.** `required = 6`, margin 3. Scores come only from counted attempts, and the scorer latches stages, so the final `score` carries every stage reached. `rollouts_exact` is computed over counted ceiling rows.

## Findings

**BLOCKING**

1. **A velocity-guard stop in `demo_replay` (and `scripted_oracle`) is an uncounted `runtime_error`, which forces the run to `inconclusive`.** In both reference arms, `robot.project_candidates` raises `ContractError("measured joint velocity limit exceeded")` (`embodiment.py:363`). The call sites are `evaluate_apple.py:2016` for replay and `:2039` for scripted. The generic handler turns the error into `runtime_error`. That is uncounted (`FAILED_TERMINATIONS`), so `replay["complete"]` becomes False and the whole reading is `inconclusive` (`object_ceiling_gate`). The same physical event in the ceiling arm is a counted `guard_refused`. It is also plausible here, not hypothetical. Replaying demonstration actions from a mismatched state is exactly what wide jitter produces, and TASK-046 attempt 43003 hit this guard at replay frame 270 (`outputs/apple-hybrid-phase-ceiling-v1`, `replay_rejection.reason`). The gate can therefore fail for a classification reason rather than a physical one. That breaks the protocol's own claim that the gate is symmetric: its list of clean terminations includes `guard_refused` without saying that the ceiling alone produces it. **Fix:** for `goal_kind == "object"` only, catch a `ContractError` whose message is in `object_ceiling.GUARD_REFUSALS` around the replay and scripted projections. End those attempts with a counted `guard_refused` and record the frame or phase and the reason. Re-raise anything else. Leave the state and hybrid `demo_replay` path byte-unchanged, so the pinned-plan test still holds. Add one focused worker test per arm. In the protocol, state that `guard_refused` applies to all three arms.
2. **The gate and the readings disagree when a ceiling attempt is uncounted.** `passed` (`object_ceiling_gate`) does not require `ceiling["complete"]`, but `conclusive` does. So with 7 counted ceiling attempts, 6 of them grasps, and replay ≤ 3, the report says `primary_gate_passed: true` together with `outcome: "inconclusive"`, whose next step is "fix the software defect and rerun". I reproduced this with the test fixtures. The frozen texts also disagree. The plan's `gate_rule` string and the manifest's `gate_rule` say "all ceiling and replay attempts counted". The protocol's primary-gate list and the code require only the replay attempts. A preregistration must not leave two contradictory verdicts for the same run. **Fix, preferred:** add `ceiling["complete"]` to `passed`, which matches the manifest, the plan string and the readings table. Add "AND all 8 `privileged_object` attempts are counted" to the protocol's gate list, plus a test (one ceiling `attempt_timeout` with 7/8 grasps gives not passed and `inconclusive`). The alternative is to state explicitly that `outcome` takes precedence over `primary_gate_passed`. Either way the change must be made before the run.

**Non-blocking**

3. **Parity can pass vacuously.** `rollouts_exact` requires zero mismatches and non-None counters, but it never checks that any checks happened. If every twin first step of a search was rejected, the next command has no check at all, silently. That includes the case where the selected command's twin first step was rejected but the live execution succeeded. With zero checks, the gate still passes (reproduced). **Fix:** require `rollout_parity_checks == rollout_full_state_parity_checks == ceiling_commands − 1` for each counted ceiling attempt with at least one command. Failing that, report both numbers per reset in the results and treat any shortfall as a software finding.
4. **The per-attempt time is tighter than the protocol states.** The earlier-version probes ran at 0.78–0.79 s/command (400 s/509, 406 s/516, 383 s/489). The final version on 42000 ran at 0.68–0.70 s. A 1,000-command attempt at 0.79 s is about 790 s plus load and retrieval, still below the effective 955 s cap (960 minus the 5 s worker reserve). Reaching 1,000 commands also needs about five stalled phases, so the risk is low. Reword "about 750 s" to "about 700–800 s". The "worst case 7,920 s" also assumes the 16 cheap attempts take about 15 s. A hung cheap attempt could take 960 s. Say "expected worst case" rather than "worst case".
5. **Infeasible candidates are still rolled out** (`object_ceiling.py`, `step` → `rollout(snapshot, projection.actions)`). This costs compute, and their first steps join the parity match set, which widens it slightly. It affects neither correctness nor the gate. Optional: pass only the feasible rows, or exclude infeasible first steps from `_search_first_steps`/`_search_full`.
6. **Test gaps.** No unit test covers: the `guard_refused` path, including the re-raise of a non-guard `ContractError`; the phase-scheduled grasp bounds (`_bounds` for each phase); the warm-start reset in `_enter`; and the acknowledgement-mismatch and clock refusals in `acknowledge`. These are cheap fixture tests. Add at least the guard one together with the fix for finding 1.
7. **Terminology.** The planner keeps a single elite, so it is an iterated elitist random search, not CEM with an elite set. The protocol's "CEM" should say "(one elite)". Separately, `_object_arm`'s `reported_ordered_stages_uncounted` is filled for counted rows too. It is harmless, but the results document should read the field as "reported stages (counted or not)".
8. **Design-probe record.** The protocol says the earlier ceiling version brought "all four corners plus 42000" to grasp and transport. `outputs/task047-scratch/` holds `run_*` logs for 42000 and three corners only. The (−3, +3 | +2, −2) earlier-version log is missing. The final version was probed on 42000 and one corner only, and the protocol says so correctly. Either point to the missing log or change the wording to three corners. Keep the scratch probe logs until the results are written.
9. **Readings wording (results obligation).** With n = 8 and a margin of 3, `separated` (for example 8 vs 5) is not statistically significant: Fisher's exact test gives p ≈ 0.2. The protocol already declares wide uncertainty. The results document must present `separated` as the preregistered decision rule for T2, not as a measured separation. Also, the conclusive predicate requires the replay arm to be complete even for the `ceiling_inadequate_*` readings. This is conservative and acceptable. State it if it happens.
10. **Run commit.** As in TASK-046, the review and any fix create a new HEAD. Run from that commit and record its SHA, noting how it differs from `73a8b0c`. Findings 1 and 2 touch `evaluate_apple.py`, so the TRAIN-42000 smoke should be re-run into a new scratch directory after the fix.

## Scientific readiness

The question is well posed and bounded. It asks whether a ±3/±2 cm distribution separates object-aware control from open-loop replay, and whether an object-aware phase cost is adequate under exact dynamics. Every arm is labelled NON-LEARNED, and learned Apple→Plate stays at 0. The new cohort is disjoint from every existing seed range. The narrow and final cohorts, TEST and TASK-034 criteria are untouched, and I verified that existing plans reproduce byte-for-byte. The design disclosure is honest on the material point. The probes covered the corners of the same box, so a ceiling pass shows adequacy on fresh draws from that box, not on a held-out distribution. The protocol says so twice. The 45000 near-centre apple was declared before any outcomes.

Apart from finding 2, the readings are complete and non-overlapping. With `ceiling["complete"]` in the gate, the outcome set becomes a partition. `inconclusive` covers incomplete or inexact runs. The remaining conclusive runs split into ceiling ≥ 6 (`separated` or `ceiling_adequate_replay_not_separated`) and ceiling < 6. The ceiling < 6 cases are then divided by the scripted reference: `task_feasible`, `scripted_also_fails` or `feasibility_reference_inconclusive`. Each reading has a concrete, separately preregistered next step, and none of them pairs the ceiling with a learned model.

After finding 1, I know of no path by which the gate can pass for a software reason. A false pass would need inexact rollouts, which the parity checks would catch (with finding 3 closing the vacuous case), or a scorer change, and the scorer is unchanged and latching. A software-driven failure is still possible through `deadline_miss`, `attempt_timeout` or "no feasible candidate". All of these are rare at the measured timings, and they surface as `inconclusive`, not as a false negative reading.
